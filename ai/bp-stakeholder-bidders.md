# Stakeholders: what bidders stand to gain

> The money is in the gap between the lowest two bids. Bot Procurement is a proposal; its test has not been run.

## In short

- Public work runs at about one win in six to ten bids, and estimating is overhead paid from the jobs won.
- On a typical public highway job the gap between the lowest and second-lowest bid is about 5 to 8 percent, about the same as a well-run contractor's profit after all costs. Bid 5 percent high and the job goes elsewhere; bid 5 percent low and the job pays nothing.
- **The pitch:** a check before the bid goes in; trust between offices; fewer wasted bids; a fair, predictable hearing; and room to set the price their own way, since a check flags a line for the firm's review and never sets it.

## Who is racing

| Bidder | What they need |
|---|---|
| **Small firm** (the whole picture fits in one head) | Nothing from a standard; an off-the-shelf AI already gives a bid to review. Not to be pressed into records it does not need. |
| **Ambitious smaller firm** stretching to a bigger job | A respectable way to confirm its own hand calculation, since it cannot ask competitors. But estimating is only part of capacity; supervision, administration and accounting must grow too, and sureties know it. |
| **Medium and large firm** | The market for the check: several estimating offices, managers who sign bids they did not build, bonding lines in the tens of millions. Needs trust between its own people, with a record of what was checked. |
| **Largest firms** | Speed without velocity: they bid and build a lot, but size and backlog plateau. |
| **Surgical teams** (in Fred Brooks's sense) | The bidder the project is for in the long run: small teams that stay principals on larger projects, without the paperwork that growth usually demands. |
| **New entrants** feeding sales material to bid-writing tools | A hearing on what they can build, not on how well their tool writes. |

## What they fear

- **The record proves what they knew.** A record that proves a clerical slip also proves the firm's bot saw an error in the drawings and the firm said nothing. Bidders will keep a record only if it is theirs and sealed, with the buyer keeping its own ([the reading record](bp-reading-record.md)).
- **Everyone leaning on the same bot.** The winner's curse depends on independent errors; a shared bot correlates them ([the competition risk](bp-competition-risk.md)).
- **Their data.** A firm's bid history is its memory; it will share the market's record, not its own.

## Aside: the surety

A seventh need is a surety that sees the difference between a firm that checked its bid and one that did not. The bonding market will be the slowest to move.
