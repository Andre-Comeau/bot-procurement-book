# The project's two names: Bot Procurement (the standard) and Bot Construction (the field)

> Bot Construction is where the work leads. Bot Procurement is what has to be earned first. Bot Procurement is a proposal; its test has not been run.

## In short

- **Bot Construction** names the field: construction work arranged, priced, administered and eventually built with bots, from documents written for machines to robots on site, built on what the trades already know.
- **Bot Procurement** names the book and the standard: the price on a tender, and who is liable when a bot gets it wrong. It is deliberately the smallest part of the field that can be tested.
- The two names are kept apart on purpose. With lawyers and insurers, the project leads with Bot Procurement.

## Why two names

A bold name draws people; a narrow one can be tested. The project wanted both. "Bot Construction" says where the work leads, which is what a field needs in order to gather people who have never met. "Bot Procurement" says what can be checked first: a bot's reading of a tender, a price, and a record. Mixing them would make the standard sound larger than it is, or the field smaller.

The rule in the book is to state the pair once, near the opening, and to keep them apart in the prose. Until the test has been run, nothing in the project calls the standard earned, tested or defended.

## The steps between them

The book sketches the road from one to the other as a series of worlds, each with its own standard to build:

1. **Bot Procurement**: pricing and checking tenders, with a reference bot and a record ([the reference bot](bp-reference-bot.md)).
2. **Documents written for machines** ("BIM for bots"): tenders and models a bot can read without guessing ([the tender template](bp-tender-template.md)).
3. **Bot administration**: the paperwork of running a job (changes, payments, records) handled by bots, with people deciding. The project prefers "administration" to "monitoring", because workers will not welcome more surveillance.
4. **Robots on site**: once the documents and the administration are machine-readable, the robots that build can be directed by other programs ([where this ends up](bp-future.md)).

## Aside: why the field is not named against anything

The economic historian Jan de Vries described a movement of historians in the twentieth century, the "Revolt of the Medievalists", that tried to dethrone the Renaissance as the start of the modern age. It failed: it was organised against an incumbent that still met a need of its day. His own field succeeded by not being a movement at all, improving the old picture rather than burying it. The project takes the lesson: Bot Construction grows out of the trades and the existing procurement protocols; it does not replace them, and its bot is a second opinion beside the engineer's estimate, not against it.
