# Stakeholders: what construction software vendors stand to gain

> Players, not referees. Today the user pays, and the vendor sells the shovel.

## In short

- **Big platforms** hold the model and the project record; their bots read PDF drawings, count, measure and spot revisions. Nothing they sell checks a price against the market.
- **Estimating specialists** sell to bidders: agents draft a first estimate from the firm's past jobs, flag every AI number, and leave the call to a person.
- **Startups** price straight from PDF plans with a general model and a supplier's price list, close to the project's own approach. None publishes how far its prices miss.
- **The bid platform:** in the United States one company sits on both sides of nearly every state highway letting and sells the states' bid data back under licence. It owns the memory; it does not price.
- **Germany's vendors** compete to read and write a public data standard, certified per program. Nobody there publishes a price error either, but the referee's whistle exists.
- **General models** with a chosen level of effort may erode the specialists' edge.

## What a published check gives them

1. **A target to build to.** A strict, public format lets a new tool compete on equal terms; a published check does the same for a bot.
2. **Demand.** Each tool sold creates a user who relies on numbers under terms that leave the risk with them, and who will want a check they can show a surety or owner. Email authentication is the precedent: when the big mail providers required it, about two million domains adopted the standard in a year.
3. **Protection from general models.** Insurance on outputs, and the ability to use numbers without being solely responsible for them, would favour tools that pass a published check ([margin insurance](bp-margin-insurance.md)).

## Aside: the data was the moat

The one head-to-head the project found comes from legal research: in 2025 a general model scored level with specialised tools on accuracy, both beat lawyers, and the specialists kept a small edge on citing the right authority from their own databases. The writing was not the moat; the data was.
