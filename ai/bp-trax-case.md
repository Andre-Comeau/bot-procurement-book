# TRAX v United States (2026): an agency's AI evaluation goes on the record

> The protest failed; the AI evaluations still went into the record. Considered AI output is part of the procurement record whether or not anyone designed for it.

## In short

- **Court:** US Court of Federal Claims, No. 26-796.
- **Stakes:** a US Army award of about $449 million.
- **Outcome:** protest denied, 22 September 2026 (opinion filed under seal earlier that month and made public that day).
- **The part that matters here:** the court ordered the agency's AI-assisted evaluations into the record.
- **Not:** a ruling on a pricing bot, on construction, or on Bot Procurement.

## Why the project watches it

The book expects the first precedents on bots in tenders to come from disputes like this, years before any standard: agent-assisted evaluations will run under old rules first. Trax shows the shape a future dispute over a pricing bot would take. A court will ask what the AI produced, what the agency considered, and whether the reasons hold up. That is a question about records ([the reading record](bp-reading-record.md)), and it is easier to answer when the bot can be rerun.

## Aside: the comparison with Canadian administrative law

Canadian Federal Court decisions on immigration officers who used software summaries have so far held that where an officer decided, the tool was "irrelevant", setting decisions aside only when the reasons failed. Procurement differs: bidders have tender-law rights and a trade tribunal, so the shield for buyers is likely weaker. *The cases are in the project's legal research notes; read them at the source before citing.*
