# Sureties and AI construction pricing: where a price check helps a bonding company, and where it does not

> A surety underwrites the firm, not the price. Bot Procurement is a proposal; its test has not been run.

## In short

- **What a surety is:** a bonding company, a company that guarantees a builder's promise to a buyer, through a bid bond and then performance and payment bonds. The contractor and usually its owners sign an indemnity agreement to repay any loss, so a surety is closer to a lender than to an insurer.
- **How well that works:** in 2023 US sureties wrote about $9.5 billion of premium with a direct loss ratio of 22.2 percent (SFAA, all surety lines, preliminary). Underwriting profit has exceeded $2 billion a year from 2022 to 2024 (AM Best).
- **What they do with a price:** after the bids open, one large surety asks its contractor for a letter when the next bid is more than 10 percent higher, with five questions (comfort with the bid, anything omitted, arithmetic errors, expected profit, the engineer's estimate). Over time, sureties track bids for patterns and ask for more frequent work-in-progress reports.
- **How often the letter would go out:** on about one highway contract in three. The project's count found the second-lowest bid more than 10 percent above the low bid on 33 percent of Texas contracts (2017 to 2024) and 35 percent of California contracts (2000 to 2008).
- **Where a check adds little:** the spread questions are already answered by estimating software, bid levelling and bid-history services, and the biggest surety losses come from growth, not estimating (the project's view).
- **The use case that stands, a proposal:** the SBA Surety Bond Guarantee Program, which guarantees 80 to 90 percent of a surety's loss on small contractors' bonds (contracts up to $9 million, $14 million on some federal work) and backed $10.6 billion of contract value in fiscal 2025, could ask for a recorded check with each guaranteed bid or charge less for checked bids.

## Why sureties lose money

In a Surety & Fidelity Association of America review of 86 claims (reported 2007), unrealistic growth was the leading factor (37 percent), including big jumps in the size of single jobs, followed by performance problems (36 percent). Estimating sat inside accounting problems (29 percent), with "bidding jobs too low" named as a warning sign. A better price does not give a firm the supervisors, managers and accountants a bigger job needs. An ambitious smaller firm stretching to a bigger job is the exception worth naming, but the surety's structural reasons for refusing still stand ([bidders](bp-stakeholder-bidders.md)).

## Slip or bet: whose side is the surety on?

In a dispute over whether a low bid was a clerical slip or a deliberate bet, the surety stands with its contractor: if the bidder proves a slip, the surety is released from the bid bond too (for example *Olympus Painting v. West Virginia DOT*, West Virginia Intermediate Court of Appeals, 2025, not final). A record that tells slips from bets protects the buyer. For the surety its use comes earlier: knowing quickly whether to back the bidder's claim or to prepare for a losing job.

## The SBA program as a buyer of the check

Here the buyer of a check would be the agency, not the surety. How much risk it removes depends on how many of the program's claims start with an underpriced bid. The program does not publish claims by cause; the newest public loss figures found are from the US Government Accountability Office for bonds issued in fiscal 1991 to 1993.
