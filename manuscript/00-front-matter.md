# Front matter

## How to read this book

This book is a manifesto with a toolkit attached. Bots are starting to read construction tenders, and the book is about the standard they will be read against: what it is for, who gains from it and what it would take.

Each part answers one question. The prologue and Parts I and II give the argument in brief: what the problem is and how we should solve it. Part III asks why other tools and other contract types are not enough. Part IV is for the people who would use the standard, one chapter for each: buyers, bidders, trades, designers and consultants, vendors, legal departments and insurers. Read your own chapter first if you are short of time. Part V is how we get there, through what AI does badly, what it does well, the tools and the standard. Parts VI and VII look back two centuries and forward to where this ends up.

Where two true statements pull against each other, both are kept and the tension is named. Construction has more tensions to manage than problems to solve, and a book that resolved them all would be a book about some other industry.

The appendices hold what a reader can skip: the history of procurement as a deck of cards, a reading list, a glossary, the bibliography and a draft term sheet. A companion edition written for AI assistants carries the sources and asides this one leaves out.

## Author's note on method

The project behind this book was run in public, and its decisions are numbered and kept. The chapters were drafted with AI assistants from my own notes and sentences, and every one has passed through my hands. This is not legal, insurance or financial advice. The legal and insurance points in this work have not been reviewed by a lawyer or an underwriter. Ask your own adviser before relying on them.
