Appendix D lists every work the book draws on. This is a shorter list, a few works for each part, for a reader who wants to go further. Full details are in Appendix D.

## For the problem

- Neil Clarke, "A Concerning Trend" (2023). An editor closes his magazine's door to a flood of machine-written stories. The scene that opens chapter 1.
- Jay Galbraith, "Organization Design: An Information Processing View" (1974). Slack or more capacity to process information, the two choices behind chapters 1 and 2.
- Anaïs Galdin and Jesse Silbert, "Making Talk Cheap" (2025). Once everyone can write well, good writing stops proving anything.
- Douglas Dyer and John Kagel, "Bidding in Common Value Auctions" (1996). How builders correct for the winner's curse.

## For the solution and the alternatives

- The Federal Highway Administration's *National Review of State Cost Estimation Practice* (2015). How far the buyer's own number misses.
- Louis Kaplow, "Rules versus Standards" (1992). When it pays to settle a question once, in advance, instead of case by case.
- Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony and Cass Sunstein, *Noise* (2021). Two experts, one file, two answers.
- The Open Contracting Data Standard (2015). Procurement's own example of an open data standard that stuck.

## For the stakeholders

- Frederick Brooks, *The Mythical Man-Month* (1975), chapter 3, "The Surgical Team". The small team as a principal.
- Tibor Scitovsky, "The Benefits of Asymmetric Markets" (1990). Who sets prices, who takes them, and what that does to a market.
- Noam Kolt, "Governing AI Agents" (2026). Why sanctions do not bite on agents, and what does.
- The Surety & Fidelity Association of America's annual results. What bonding companies lose, and why.

## For the way there

- David Lang, "Standards Make the World" (2023). Standards are made by small groups of people.
- Nadia Eghbal, *Working in Public* (2020). How open projects are kept alive.
- Andrew Russell, "OSI: The Internet That Wasn't" (2013). A formal standard that lost to running code.
- ISO 10845, *Construction procurement*. The international standard this project would add to.

## For the past and the future

- Venkatesh Rao, "New Nature" (2026). Laws made by technology that are nearly as hard to appeal as nature's.
- Aneesh Sathe, *Prime Radiant* and the World Machines project. The model for the Procurement Machines cards.
- *The Code of Hammurabi*. Where the builder's liability starts.
