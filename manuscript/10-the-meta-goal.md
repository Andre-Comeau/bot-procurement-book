### The meta-goal as first stated: worker autonomy

> The meta-goal of the study is increased autonomy for workers. Taylorism split the worker from the manager, with measurable productivity gains, but these gains have plateaued, so a new paradigm is needed. AI provides the means to build one, by empowering workers to self-perform a wide array of administrative tasks that currently siphon productivity towards staff positions. It may not revolutionize the entire industry, but it should allow more workers who are currently discouraged from principal-level work by unfamiliarity with documentation to step into principal roles.

(`case-studies/andre-comeau/README.md`, "The meta-goal")

### Where value moves

*Claude's draft (THESIS section 4): the work goes to the* keyholder*, who signs the number and adjusts the flagged lines; to the* data steward*, who keeps a firm's own history usable by its own Witness; and to the* standards author*, because the standard tender format is the precondition for everything above. "This is also the author's meta-goal in another form: the tools remove the documentation barrier that kept workers from principal roles, and what remains is judgment about ends."*

### From procurement to robots

> What would happen if we extended the vision to include BIM modelling and robotics? BIM modelling: underused by humans, ripe for bot usage as a purely digital model. Robotics: robot control as an extension of BIM. Both these could see higher adaptation if bots were in higher usage in construction with more common standards for producing and using the information.

(positions §24, recorded 2026-10-17)

### Appeal as a human activity

> I am not sure how this thinking will fit into the Bot Procurement mission. It feels philosophical, but it also points to the limits of the system. With the real-world complexity of construction, it feels unlikely that any of the parties would want to waive their rights to appeal. (They would all be willing to see their counterparties lose these rights.) Perhaps developing "rights-to-appeal" is a fundamentally human activity? The foundation of religious thought: God as a source of appeal against the unappealable force of nature?
