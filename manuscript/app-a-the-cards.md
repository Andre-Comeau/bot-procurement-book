# Appendix A. Procurement Machines: the cards

The history in chapter 18 is told in full as a deck of cards. Each card is a procurement machine: a way of buying building work that people built, relied on and sometimes abandoned. The deck runs from Babylon to 2026 and is published as the Procurement Machines site, at [machines.andrecomeau.xyz](https://machines.andrecomeau.xyz).

The deck is bonus material. A reader who skips it loses nothing from the argument, and a reader who stays gets the history behind it. It began as a side project to map where procurement came from and to see whether listing each machine's inputs and outputs could hint at the machines still to come.

## How to read a card

A machine has inputs, outputs, a purpose and links to the machines around it. It also has the ways it tends to fail, and a life: when it was built, when it ran and whether it is still running.

Each card names the tensions that hold the machine together, the party on the other side of each one, and the signal that would show it breaking.

An event card marks a place where several machines meet at once. It makes predictions, and they can be wrong. The Army case of chapter 1 is the only one so far.

A contraption is a quick assembly built in a gap between machines. Most collapse. A few find structure and become machines. The watchlist follows them, including this project's own estimator.

## The thread

Eight cards carry the argument in order, and they are the best way into the deck.

1. About 1750 BCE, the builder's liability. The builder answers for what falls down and cannot appeal.
2. About 495 BCE, the public letting. Rome lets the building of a temple to contractors, and guarantors and a public record follow over the centuries after.
3. 1809, formal advertising. Federal purchases must be advertised for proposals, the procedure that became the sealed bid.
4. 1873, the price book. Published cost data give the owner a number before the market speaks.
5. 1921, the bid protest and the record. An award is tested against the file the agency made at the time.
6. 2006, the machine auditors. The first machines to take part in the loop fall out of use wherever nobody has to act on their flags.
7. 2026, the Army case. An agency's AI reaches a court.
8. Projected, the bot-first protocol. The machine that does not exist yet.
