# Appendix F. Who to talk to first

This appendix goes with the stakeholder chapters, 7 to 13. It names the kinds of people and the bodies the project would approach first, and what it would ask each of them. It holds no names of individuals and no contact details. Nobody listed here has been approached about the standard, and none of them endorses it.

The order matters. The first conversations are the ones that can prove the project wrong cheaply: the people who hold the data, then the people who would price the risk, then the bodies that would carry the standard.

## First: the data and the test

| Who | Why first | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| A state transportation department that publishes every bid, such as Texas | Its bid tabulations are the market memory the estimator learns from and the test is scored against | Confirm the licence for reuse, and whether newer lettings can be held back as a fresh test |
| Bidders on civil work, small and medium | They are the primary users, and only their books can show whether a margin really fell short | Run the estimator beside their own estimate on live tenders, and share the outcome on jobs won |
| Estimating societies, such as AACE International and the Canadian Institute of Quantity Surveyors | They already publish how accurate an estimate should be at each stage | Comment on the tool test as a conformance class beside the estimate classes |

## Second: the people who would price the risk

| Who | Why | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| A reinsurer already insuring AI performance, such as Munich Re | It pays claims on a model's performance without anyone proving negligence | Guidance on how an open standard should be written so that software built to it can be underwritten (after the book is complete, through its public form) |
| A bonding broker, and the surety associations in the United States and Canada | They already read a bid spread and ask for a letter when it is wide | Whether margin cover could sit beside the bond, placed by the same broker at award |
| A software vendor with its own insurance brokerage | It already sells cover only to its own users, priced from its data | Whether cover on the bot's price, sold with the bot, would fit its programs (chapter 11) |

## Third: the bodies that would carry the standards

| Who | Which standard | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| The Canadian Construction Documents Committee | Documents | A note in CCDC 23 on issuing the template beside the PDF and naming which governs |
| buildingSMART International | Documents | How the template's requirement identifiers could tie to IDS and IFC 4.3 |
| The Open Contracting Partnership | Sign-off | An extension for signed readings, beside the contract data governments already publish |
| ISO TC 59/SC 18, through the Standards Council of Canada | Tool test | Whether a part of ISO 10845 could hold a public test set and tolerance |
| RICS, and engineering regulators | Sign-off | Whether their AI practice duties could name a reading signed against the tool test |
| A public owner willing to pilot | All three | One tender issued with the template beside the PDF, with every reading signed (chapter 17, version 2.0) |

## What to bring

Each conversation needs the same three things: the book, the estimator's rehearsal scores with their limits stated, and the draft term sheet in Appendix E. Each one ends with the same question: what would it take for you to say no?
